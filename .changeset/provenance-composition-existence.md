Provenance support treats a required composition part as dependent on its whole, so a belief-status close reaches the parts that cannot exist without it. A fact whose kind is an `existence: "required"` part is supported only while the whole it currently hangs from is itself supported (a whole that is also a fact kind) or a live row (any other whole). Which whole a part hangs from is read through the same page-scoped owner `store.verifyConstraintFences()`' `compositionExistence` family reads, and a non-fact whole's liveness through the same verdict the write path's whole-endpoint refusal applies, so provenance re-spells no composition orientation, population predicate, or liveness rule of its own. Closing a whole's currency therefore closes its required parts in the same transition, transitively through a part that is itself a whole, and `RetractionReport.died` names every one of them; the affected set is widened along the same dependency so `survivedVia` and `unaffected` stay accurate. Reopening the whole reopens the parts that are otherwise supported — reopen remains support-driven, so there is no ledger of what a close closed — and a required part is closed even when a different source supports it, because the existence dependency dominates its own grounding.

A non-fact whole whose validity window has ENDED still holds its parts up: it is a live row, and the write path would still accept it as a whole. Each closed fact fires its own `delete` operation hook, parts included, rather than folding into one event the way the delete cascade's single hook does — every part reaching the close is a fact of its own, closed by its own support verdict.

An optional part is untouched: it can exist with no whole, so it keeps its attachment to the closed whole and its own belief status, and the composition claim still stops a second whole from taking it. No edge is deleted by any of this, so the `compositionExistence` family reports nothing after a close — a closed required part is tombstoned, not an orphan. A graph that declares no required part pays no extra read.

`createRetractionCapability` now refuses a configuration whose fact kinds own required parts it cannot reach: a fact kind that is the whole of an `existence: "required"` pair whose part kind is not itself a fact kind throws `ConfigurationError` (`PROVENANCE_REQUIRED_PART_NOT_A_FACT`, naming the whole kind, the part kind, and every offending pair). Both sides are read through subsumption, so a fact kind that subclasses a declared whole kind counts, and so does a subclass of a declared required part kind.

`store.verifyConstraintFences()`' `compositionExistence` family now also reports a live required part whose whole is TOMBSTONED, not only one with no composition edge at all — a state the delete cascade cannot produce but a direct backend write, a custom port, or a bypassed import can. The write path's incumbent decision is unchanged and deliberately narrower: a tombstoned whole still holds its part's attachment there, so a second whole cannot quietly take it. Each page of the audit reads its wholes once per kind through the batch point read, falling back to the per-id read that bundle declares.

### Breaking

- `retract` / `retractMany` now close the required composition parts of any whole they close, and `unRetract` / `unRetractMany` reopen the ones that regained support. A caller that relied on a closed whole keeping its required parts believed sees them tombstoned, listed in `RetractionReport.died`.
- `createRetractionCapability` throws `PROVENANCE_REQUIRED_PART_NOT_A_FACT` for a configuration it previously accepted, when a fact kind owns a required composition part that is not a fact kind.
- `store.verifyConstraintFences()` reports a `compositionExistence` violation for a live required part attached to a tombstoned whole, which previously read clean.
- `GraphReadBackend` gains the optional `getNodes` member it already had on `GraphBackend`, so a read surface narrowed by hand can now pass the batch point read through.
