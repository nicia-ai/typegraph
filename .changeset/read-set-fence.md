Add `tx.assertUnchangedSince(anchor, scopes)`, a read-set fence for callers that read, compute for a while, then write a result derived from what they read. Capture `store.lineageRevisionNow()` before reading, then declare what was read as nodes, edges, edge neighbourhoods, node or edge kinds, or the whole graph. Inside the write transaction the store takes its write lock, then refuses with `ReadSetChangedError` (listing each changed scope with up to 100 node and 100 edge keys, and per-list truncation flags) if anything declared has changed, or `ReadSetUnverifiableError` if the lineage source cannot prove the span. It needs a store with `history: true` or `revisionTracking: true` and, on PostgreSQL, read committed isolation; otherwise it raises `UnsupportedBackendCapabilityError` naming `lineage`, `graphWriteFence` or `observedReadCommittedIsolation`. A history store answers each scope exactly (row and neighbourhood scopes by index seek, kind and whole-graph scopes from the recorded history they cover); a `revisionTracking` store without history answers an edge-neighbourhood scope at edge-kind granularity, because its journal records no endpoints. Identity assertions are not covered and do not refuse the fence on either store type. Graph-merge's write-fence prelude moved to a shared store-level owner with no change in merge behavior.
