The provenance retraction verbs take an optional transaction as their last argument: `retract(source, tx)`, `retractMany(sources, tx)`, `unRetract(source, tx)` and `unRetractMany(sources, tx)`. With it, the transition runs on the caller's transaction instead of opening its own, so a retraction and the writes that depend on it (the caller's own rows, an edge the retracted claim stood for) commit or roll back as one unit. The verb takes the schema-version fence and then the graph write lock before its first read, as it does in its own transaction, and neither commits nor rolls back. Its writes are visible to the transaction's later reads, `holding(tx)` and `assertHolds(tx, …)`, are counted in the caller's receipt and `tx.measure` scope, and are recorded at the caller's recorded revision. The report and the order `retractMany` / `unRetractMany` apply sources in are the same with and without a transaction. Calls without a transaction behave as before.

With engine-native recorded time on PostgreSQL, an ordinary write takes no graph lock, so a verb called after one in the same transaction takes the graph lock after that write's row locks, the reverse of a concurrent retraction; PostgreSQL can then abort one of the two with a deadlock, surfaced as the retryable `TransactionConflictError`, as `assertHolds(tx, …)` already could. Call the verb before the transaction's other writes to avoid it.

Hooks follow the hook contract of the context passed: in `store.transaction()` and `transactionWithReceipt()` the verb's `onOperationEnd` hooks fire at the caller's commit and not at all on rollback; in an adopted `withRecordedTransaction()` or `withEvolvedTransaction()` context they fire as each write completes, before the native commit, so they can report a retraction the native caller then rolls back.

A transaction from another store or graph, or one kept past its callback, is refused with `ConfigurationError` (`PROVENANCE_TRANSACTION_NOT_OWNED`); a second argument that is not a transaction context at all with `ConfigurationError` (`PROVENANCE_TRANSACTION_ARGUMENT_INVALID`), naming the value received; a read-only transaction with `UnsupportedBackendCapabilityError` before any statement; on a keyed write fence (PostgreSQL's `advisory` default, or `row`), a transaction whose graph-lock statement observed an isolation other than read committed, or could not report one, with `UnsupportedBackendCapabilityError` (`observedReadCommittedIsolation`) before the first support read, since its snapshot could predate another session's retraction (a write fence that serializes writers, `engine-serialized` or `caller-serialized`, needs no such evidence); a stale store handle with `StaleVersionError`. `holding(tx)` and `assertHolds(tx, …)` refuse a non-context argument the same way. On a SQLite transaction adopted through `withRecordedTransaction()` or `withEvolvedTransaction()`, which may have been begun `DEFERRED`, the verb takes the writer slot before its first support read, and a transaction whose snapshot another connection overtook is refused with `TransactionConflictError` before anything is read. A failure inside the verb takes no savepoint: if the caller catches it and commits, the writes the verb made before failing commit too. Every verb now closes the facts that lose their support before it updates the source flags, and reopens the facts that regain support after, so such a prefix never leaves a fact live without live support; running the verb again completes it. The order of the verb's operation hooks changes accordingly. In an adopted context a rejected callback must be followed by a rollback of the native transaction: committing it instead commits the callback's writes, a retraction's included, to the live rows, and under TypeGraph-owned history capture leaves them out of recorded time (issue #930); a backend with engine-native recorded time records them itself.

### Breaking

- Passing a verb directly to an array method no longer works: `sources.map(provenance.retract)` passes each element's index as the transaction, which is a TypeScript error and, in JavaScript, is refused with `PROVENANCE_TRANSACTION_ARGUMENT_INVALID`. Pass a function, `sources.map((source) => provenance.retract(source))`, or retract the sources as one transition with `retractMany(sources)`. The same applies to `retractMany`, `unRetract` and `unRetractMany`.
