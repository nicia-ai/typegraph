The provenance retraction verbs take an optional transaction as their last argument: `retract(source, tx)`, `retractMany(sources, tx)`, `unRetract(source, tx)` and `unRetractMany(sources, tx)`. With it, the transition runs on the caller's transaction instead of opening its own, so a retraction and the writes that depend on it (the caller's own rows, an edge the retracted claim stood for) commit or roll back as one unit. The verb takes the schema-version fence and then the graph write lock before its first read, as it does in its own transaction, and neither commits nor rolls back. Its writes are visible to the transaction's later reads, `holding(tx)` and `assertHolds(tx, …)`, its hooks fire at the caller's commit, it is counted in the caller's receipt and `tx.measure` scope, and it is recorded at the caller's recorded revision. The report and the order `retractMany` / `unRetractMany` apply sources in are the same with and without a transaction. A transaction from another store or graph, or one kept past its callback, is refused with `ConfigurationError` (`PROVENANCE_TRANSACTION_NOT_OWNED`); a read-only one with `UnsupportedBackendCapabilityError` before any statement; a stale store handle with `StaleVersionError`. A failure inside the verb takes no savepoint: if the caller catches it and commits, the writes the verb made before failing commit too. Calls without a transaction behave as before.
