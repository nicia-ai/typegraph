**Provenance support treats a required composition part as dependent on its whole**, so a belief-status close reaches the parts that cannot exist without it. A fact whose kind is an `existence: "required"` part is supported only while the whole it currently hangs from is itself supported (a whole that is also a fact kind) or a live row (any other whole). Closing a whole's currency therefore closes its required parts in the same transition, transitively through a part that is itself a whole; `RetractionReport.died` names every one of them, and `survivedVia` and `unaffected` stay accurate because the affected set widens along the same dependency. Reopening the whole reopens the parts that are otherwise supported, reopen remaining support-driven rather than a ledger of what a close closed, and a required part is closed even when a different source supports it, because the existence dependency dominates its own grounding.

A non-fact whole whose validity window has ENDED still holds its parts up: it is a live row, and the write path would still accept it as a whole. Each closed fact fires its own `delete` operation hook, parts included, rather than folding into one event the way the delete cascade's single hook does. An optional part is untouched: it can exist with no whole, so it keeps its attachment to the closed whole and its own belief status, and the composition claim still stops a second whole from taking it. No edge is deleted by any of this, so the `compositionExistence` audit family reports nothing after a close — a closed required part is tombstoned, not an orphan. A graph that declares no required part pays no extra read.

**`RetractionReport.died` now names every fact a transition closes**, not only those believed before it. A live fact is unsupported whenever no justification of it fires — reachable through ordinary store calls, for example a justification one of whose premises is a source created with its retracted flag already set, which leaves the derived fact live and unsupported with no constraint violated — and a transition that reaches such a fact closes it, so it is reported in `died`. The composition case is narrower: a required part whose whole was tombstoned outside the store's own paths, which `store.verifyConstraintFences()` also reports.

**`createRetractionCapability` refuses a configuration whose fact kinds own required parts it cannot reach**: a fact kind that is the whole of an `existence: "required"` pair whose part kind is not itself a fact kind throws `ConfigurationError` (`PROVENANCE_REQUIRED_PART_NOT_A_FACT`, naming the whole kind, the part kind and every offending pair). Both sides are read through subsumption, so a fact kind that subclasses a declared whole kind counts, and so does a subclass of a declared required part kind.

**The audit sees one more orphan shape.** `store.verifyConstraintFences()`' `compositionExistence` family also reports a live required part whose whole is TOMBSTONED, not only one with no composition edge at all — a state the delete cascade cannot produce but a direct backend write, a custom port or a bypassed import can. The write path's incumbent decision is unchanged and deliberately narrower: a tombstoned whole still holds its part's attachment there, so a second whole cannot quietly take it.

### Breaking

- `RetractionReport.died` can name a fact that was not believed before the transition, when that fact was live, unsupported and closed by it. A consumer treating `died` as a subset of the previously believed set sees an extra entry instead of a silent tombstone.
- `retract` / `retractMany` close the required composition parts of any whole they close, and `unRetract` / `unRetractMany` reopen the ones that regained support. A caller that relied on a closed whole keeping its required parts believed sees them tombstoned, listed in `RetractionReport.died`.
- `createRetractionCapability` throws `PROVENANCE_REQUIRED_PART_NOT_A_FACT` for a configuration it previously accepted. Declare the part kind a fact kind, or move the relation off `existence: "required"`.
- `store.verifyConstraintFences()` reports a `compositionExistence` violation for a live required part attached to a tombstoned whole, which previously read clean.
- `GraphReadBackend` gains the optional `getNodes` member it already had on `GraphBackend`, so a read surface narrowed by hand can pass the batch point read through.
